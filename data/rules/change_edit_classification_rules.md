# Change or Edit Classification

In the context of requirements engineering, it is crucial to distinguish between high-impact and low-impact edits to ensure proper assessment of the implications and effort involved in managing changes. Below is a detailed explanation of the two categories of edits.

## High-impact Edits

**Definition:**  
High-impact edits are modifications that significantly alter the system’s expected behavior or introduce changes to its semantics. These edits affect the core functionality or logical flow of the system, requiring careful consideration and potential updates to multiple related components, processes, or documentation.

**Examples:**  
1. **Addition or Removal of Requirements:**  
   Introducing a new requirement or removing an existing one.  
   _Example:_ Adding a requirement for a new payment gateway.

2. **Addition or Removal of Features:**  
   Incorporating a new feature or deprecating an existing one.  
   _Example:_ Introducing a new search filter in an e-commerce platform.

3. **Modifications to Business Model Entities:**  
   Changing the structure or scope of entities such as roles, permissions, or stakeholders.  
   _Example:_ Adding a new user role with specific privileges.

4. **Updates to System Flows:**  
   Altering main steps, exception flows, or alternative flows.  
   _Example:_ Modifying a checkout process to include a new step for gift wrapping options.

**Impact:**  
High-impact edits typically require reevaluation of system design, additional development effort, regression testing, and updates to user documentation. These changes may also affect project timelines and resource allocation.

---

## Low-impact Edits

**Definition:**  
Low-impact edits are minor changes that do not affect the behavior or functionality of the system. These edits are typically syntactic in nature, focusing on improving clarity, consistency, or presentation of the requirements without altering their meaning or intent.

**Examples:**  
1. **Textual Updates:**  
   Correcting typos, grammar, or spelling errors.  
   _Example:_ Fixing "Recieve" to "Receive."

2. **Rephrasing for Clarity:**  
   Using synonyms or rewording a requirement for better understanding.  
   _Example:_ Changing "The system shall allow" to "The system must permit."

3. **Formatting and Punctuation:**  
   Adjusting layout, punctuation, or stylistic elements.  
   _Example:_ Adding bullet points to a list for readability.

4. **Structural Adjustments:**  
   Reordering or renaming sections without altering their content.  
   _Example:_ Moving "Authentication" requirements to precede "Authorization."

5. **Usability Enhancements:**  
   Adding or removing elements for improved user experience, added or removed from the screen (for display, review, or verification purposes) 
   _Example:_ Including tooltips for form fields.
   _Example:_ A table was presented before, now a table and an explanatory text below it are presented.

**Impact:**  
Low-impact edits generally do not require system-level changes or extensive testing. They can often be implemented quickly and independently of other tasks, focusing solely on improving documentation quality.

---

**Summary:**  
Understanding the distinction between high- and low-impact edits is essential for effective requirements management. High-impact edits necessitate comprehensive evaluation and coordination, whereas low-impact edits can be addressed as part of routine document maintenance.
